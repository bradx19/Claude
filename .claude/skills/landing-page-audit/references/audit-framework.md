# Conversion Audit Framework

## Scoring Rubrics

### 1. Headline Clarity (20% weight)

**What to evaluate**: Does the headline immediately communicate who this is for, what they get, and why it matters?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Instantly clear target audience, specific benefit, compelling reason to care. No jargon. |
| 7-8 | Clear value proposition, identifies audience or benefit but not both perfectly. |
| 5-6 | Generic benefit statement, unclear who it's for, requires reading subhead to understand. |
| 3-4 | Vague, feature-focused, or clever/cute without clarity. |
| 1-2 | Confusing, misleading, or completely absent. |

**Evidence to collect**:
- H1 text verbatim
- Subheadline text
- Above-the-fold messaging
- Time to understand value (immediate vs requires scrolling)

### 2. CTA Strength (25% weight)

**What to evaluate**: Are CTAs prominent and action-oriented? Do they create urgency and reduce friction?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | High-contrast button, action verb, clear next step, visible without scrolling, repeated strategically. |
| 7-8 | Clear CTA with good placement, minor issues with contrast or copy. |
| 5-6 | CTA exists but competes with other elements, generic text ("Submit", "Learn More"). |
| 3-4 | Hard to find, low contrast, passive language, buried below fold. |
| 1-2 | No clear CTA or multiple competing CTAs causing confusion. |

**Evidence to collect**:
- Primary CTA button text
- CTA placement (above fold, multiple locations)
- Button color/contrast
- Supporting microcopy
- Number of CTAs and consistency

### 3. Objection Handling (20% weight)

**What to evaluate**: Does the page proactively address reasons someone might hesitate?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Addresses price, trust, time, complexity objections. FAQ section, guarantees, risk reversals. |
| 7-8 | Handles 2-3 major objections clearly, some gaps remain. |
| 5-6 | Basic trust signals present but doesn't proactively address objections. |
| 3-4 | Minimal objection handling, leaves major concerns unaddressed. |
| 1-2 | No attempt to reduce friction or address hesitations. |

**Common objections to look for**:
- Price/value concerns
- Trust/credibility questions
- Time investment required
- Technical complexity
- Comparison to alternatives
- Risk/commitment level

**Evidence to collect**:
- FAQ presence and content
- Guarantees (money-back, free trial)
- Trust badges (security, certifications)
- Pricing transparency
- Risk-reduction language

### 4. Social Proof Placement (15% weight)

**What to evaluate**: Is credibility demonstrated through third-party validation?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Multiple proof types (testimonials, logos, stats, reviews), strategically placed near CTAs. |
| 7-8 | Good social proof present, could be better positioned or more specific. |
| 5-6 | Generic testimonials or logos present but not compelling. |
| 3-4 | Minimal proof, buried at bottom, or appears fabricated. |
| 1-2 | No social proof or only self-promotional claims. |

**Types of social proof**:
- Customer testimonials (with names, photos, specifics)
- Company logos ("Trusted by...")
- Usage statistics ("10,000+ customers")
- Third-party reviews/ratings
- Case studies/results
- Media mentions
- Certifications/awards

**Evidence to collect**:
- Types of social proof present
- Placement relative to CTAs
- Specificity of claims
- Credibility indicators (real names, photos, verifiable)

### 5. Persuasion Architecture (20% weight)

**What to evaluate**: Does the page guide visitors through a logical journey from problem to action?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Clear problem-agitate-solution flow, logical section progression, builds to CTA naturally. |
| 7-8 | Good structure with minor flow issues, most sections support the conversion goal. |
| 5-6 | Sections present but disjointed, unclear narrative arc. |
| 3-4 | Random content arrangement, hard to follow, sections work against each other. |
| 1-2 | No discernible structure, overwhelming or confusing layout. |

**Ideal structure patterns**:
1. Problem identification
2. Solution introduction
3. How it works
4. Benefits/features
5. Social proof
6. Objection handling
7. Clear CTA

**Evidence to collect**:
- Section order and flow
- Narrative coherence
- Visual hierarchy
- Scroll depth to key information
- Mobile vs desktop structure

## Calculating Weighted Score

```
Overall = (Headline * 0.20) + (CTA * 0.25) + (Objections * 0.20) + (Social * 0.15) + (Architecture * 0.20)
```

**Score interpretation**:
- 8.0-10.0: Excellent - Optimized for conversion
- 6.0-7.9: Good - Solid foundation with improvement opportunities
- 4.0-5.9: Average - Significant gaps affecting conversion
- Below 4.0: Poor - Major overhaul needed

## Competitive Gap Analysis

When comparing target vs competitors:

1. **Identify largest score gaps** - Where does target underperform by 2+ points?
2. **Find competitor strengths** - What are competitors doing well that target isn't?
3. **Spot opportunities** - Where are all pages weak? First-mover advantage opportunity.
4. **Prioritize by weight** - CTA gaps (25%) matter more than Social Proof gaps (15%).
