# Anti-AI Writing

Make a draft read like the agent wrote it, not a chatbot. Keep what it says. Don't add anything.

AI picks the safest, most common choice for every sentence, so its writing sounds like everyone's. A person writes for one reader about one thing. Each pattern below is a safe default to replace with a specific choice.

## Two rules first

- The team's brand voice skill wins. If it calls for emojis, exclamation points, dashes, a signature phrase or a sign-off, keep them, even where a pattern below says cut.
- Never add a fact. No new price, feature, stat, date or claim. If a sentence needs a detail you don't have, write a simpler sentence or leave a [blank] for the agent.

## How to work

Read the whole draft, fix the patterns, then read it once more for the ones that most often survive a rewrite (1, 2, 7 and 11). Rewrite each sentence around its point instead of swapping single words. Mix short and long sentences.

Return only the final text. Show what you changed only if asked.

## Patterns, strongest first

1. **Not X, but Y.** "It's not just a house, it's a lifestyle." The fake contrast adds weight, not information. Say the point.
   - Before: This isn't just a home, it's a retreat.
   - After: The back deck faces the mountains and gets afternoon shade.

2. **Closers and fragments.** A last line that repeats the point ("Don't miss this one!", "This is the one."), a row of fragments ("Mountain views. Private road. No HOA."), or a lesson tacked on after a fact. Cut it, or turn the fragments into one sentence.

3. **Warm-up before the point.** "Great question!", "Thanks so much for reaching out!", "I'd be happy to help!", "Here's what you need to know." In a lead reply, the first sentence answers the question.
   - Before: Hi Sarah! Thanks so much for reaching out! I'd be happy to help with your question about 123 Oak. Yes, it's still available!
   - After: Hi Sarah, yes, 123 Oak is still available. Want to see it Thursday or Saturday?

4. **Chatbot leftovers.** "I hope this helps," "Feel free to reach out with any questions," "Certainly!", "Let me know if there's anything else I can do." Cut them unless the brand voice uses one as the team's sign-off.

5. **Real estate sales words.** nestled, boasts, stunning, breathtaking, dream home, oasis, hidden gem, rare opportunity, look no further, won't last long, entertainer's dream, perfect blend, sanctuary, in the heart of, must-see. Say what the thing is.
   - Before: Nestled in the heart of Sevierville, this stunning 3-bedroom gem boasts breathtaking mountain views and an entertainer's dream kitchen.
   - After: This 3-bedroom in Sevierville has mountain views and a kitchen with [standout detail].

6. **Stock AI words.** additionally, crucial, delve, enhance, elevate, key (as in "key features"), landscape ("the market landscape"), navigate ("navigate the market"), seamless, showcase, testament, vibrant, robust. One alone is fine. Several together are a tell.

7. **Threes for rhythm.** "Spacious, bright, and modern." Keep a list of three only when there are three real things worth naming. Listing descriptions are full of these.

8. **Big words for small facts.** "A pivotal time in the market," "the market continues to evolve," "exciting times ahead," "now more than ever." Keep the fact and drop the drama. End a market update on the last real number.
   - Before: The Sevierville market continues to evolve, making now a pivotal time for buyers.
   - After: Sevierville had [number] new listings this week, up from [number] last week.

9. **Fancy verbs.** boasts, features, offers, serves as, showcases. Use has and is.

10. **-ing add-ons.** "...offering endless possibilities," "...ensuring a smooth closing," "...making it perfect for families." Cut the add-on or state the real fact. Describe the home, not the buyer; "perfect for families" is also a Fair Housing problem.

11. **Dashes.** Replace em dashes with periods, commas or colons, unless the brand voice uses them.

12. **Decoration.** Bold words, Title Case Headings, emojis as bullets, arrows. Remove them unless the channel and the brand voice use them (Instagram often does).

13. **Borrowed authority and guesses.** "Experts say now is the time to buy," "as of my last update," "based on available data, it's likely..." Name the real source or cut the claim. Never fill a gap with a guess.

14. **Over-explaining a reply.** A lead reply that restates the question, explains, then answers last. Put the answer and the next step first. Cut background the lead already knows.

## Leave alone

- Anything the brand voice skill lists under how the team sounds, or their words and habits.
- Real, specific details: a street name, a neighbor's story, an odd fact only a local would know.
- Normal greetings and sign-offs ("Hi Sarah," "Talk soon, Jared").
- Quotes from clients or reviews.
- One pattern alone in otherwise human writing. Several together are the signal.
